### Fixed

- **simulation**: `replay()` paces recorded frames on `time.monotonic()`. The pacer read `time.time()` twice per frame and slept for the remainder of the frame interval, so a wall-clock step mid-episode - an NTP correction, a `date -s`, a resume from suspend - changed the rate at which a recorded trajectory was sent to the robot: forward, the subtraction went negative, the sleep was skipped and every remaining frame was pushed as fast as the loop could turn over; backward, the sleep became `frame_interval + step` and the replay stalled with the arm parked on a recorded pose. Both finished `Frames: N/N` with `status="success"`, and the `duration_s` they reported moved with the step too. The rollout duration `run()` reports as `elapsed_s` was on the same clock.
- **policies/lerobot_local**: inference latency and the two model-load timings are measured on `time.monotonic()`. The latency is not only telemetry: it is the window `_estimate_inference_delay` takes a p95 of to produce the RTC inference delay, which freezes that many actions of each new chunk to the already-committed prefix and is the offset the chunk-seam slice skips. A wall-clock step was therefore recorded as one inference that took the size of the step, and a lone outlier is the p95 of a window holding 2..20 samples - the range every episode starts in, because `reset()` clears it - so one correction discarded the newly computed chunk at roughly the next 19 seams while the arm went on executing the stale one, then recovered by itself with nothing raised. `load_time_s` could likewise be reported negative or as hours for a load a correction landed in.
