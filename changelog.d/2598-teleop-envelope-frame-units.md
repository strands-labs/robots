### Fixed

- **mesh**: the teleop input envelope is now sized in the unit the frames carry. `DEFAULT_INPUT_VALUE_ABS` and `DEFAULT_INPUT_SLEW_ABS` are compared against whatever the leader driver puts on the wire, and both were sized for radians -- but lerobot's SO leader/follower default to `use_degrees=True` and their gripper is `MotorNormMode.RANGE_0_100` whatever that flag says, so a shipped SO-100 class arm streams degrees and a 0-100 gripper and no configuration of it fits. Measured on the real validator, 45 degrees of shoulder pan and a half-open gripper were both refused, leaving a usable reach of ~12.6 units on joints whose travel is measured in hundreds. The slew bound's own docstring is the proof: it derives its margin as "roughly 4x the no-load speed of the Feetech STS3215 ... (~6.5 rad/s)", which is self-consistent only in radians -- in the frame unit that servo produces ~372 deg/s, so the bound sat an order of magnitude *below* the leader's own speed and its claim that "a physical leader arm cannot reach it" was inverted. Both defaults are converted rather than retuned, preserving the policy they encode (two full turns of reach; that envelope traversed once per second), which reproduces the documented "roughly 4x" margin in the right unit. A fleet on a smaller unit (radians, or normalized -1..1) now narrows via `STRANDS_MESH_INPUT_VALUE_ABS` / `STRANDS_MESH_INPUT_SLEW_ABS` instead of widening. README's configuration table documented the retired radian values and named the unit as radians, so it is corrected in the same change and now graded against the constants themselves, and the local `teleoperate()` loop's comment no longer justifies its own bound by calling the mesh one radian-scoped -- the mesh bound is now the looser of the two.
