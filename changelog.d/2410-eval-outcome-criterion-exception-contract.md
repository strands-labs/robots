### Fixed

- **simulation**: a raising episode-outcome criterion now names what failed. Both eval routes call a caller-supplied criterion after every applied action (`eval_policy`'s `success_fn`, a benchmark spec's `is_success` / `is_failure`), and every other per-step hook on those paths already stated what a raise means - a generic `on_frame` failure is best-effort telemetry, a `RecordingFrameError` from it is data loss and propagates, `spec.on_step` returns `status="error"` naming the hook, and `run`'s `stop_when` is fatal with a message naming the step. The outcome criterion had none, so it arrived as the caller's own bare exception from inside a nested loop, naming neither the criterion, the episode nor the step - and a criterion that only breaks on a later episode cost the whole evaluation without saying why. The failure now carries all three and chains the original. Verdicts are coerced with `bool()` rather than type-checked, so the `numpy.bool_` an ordinary predicate returns is still accepted, and each method keeps its own surface (`run` converts via its terminal handler; `evaluate` propagates rollout failures by design).
